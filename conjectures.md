## Number Theory

**Conjecture 1 (Universal Emission):**  
For every odd integer $n > 1$, there exists an even integer $e$, with $0 < e < n$, such that both $n - e$ and $n + e$ are primes.

**Conjecture 2 (Simplex Unsieveability):**  
Let $S_k(n)$ denote the sum of k consecutive integers starting at $n$, i.e., $S_k(n) = n + (n+1) + … + (n+k-1)$.
Then, for every prime $p > k$, there does not exist an integer $n$ such that $S_k(n) = p$, except possibly for finitely many $p$.

Special case, $k = 3$: For all primes $p > 3$, there is no $n$ such that $p = n + (n+1) + (n+2)$.

**Conjecture 3 (Disjoint Modular Emission Chains):**  
Define an emission chain as the sequence of primes generated by repeatedly applying the symmetric emission rule (from Conjecture 1) within a fixed modulus m residue class.
Then, for modulus $m = 4$, the emission chains with starting residue classes 1 mod 4 and 3 mod 4 remain disjoint for all iterations.

**Conjecture 4 (Last-Digit Pair Symmetry):**  
In the sequence of prime emissions produced by the symmetric rule (Conjecture 1), the pairs $(n-e, n+e)$ preferentially appear such that their decimal units digits are either both 3 and 7, or both 1 and 9, or are equal and separated by a gap that is a multiple of 10.

**Conjecture 5 (Emission Ratio Law):**  
For all but finitely many odd $n > 1$ and their corresponding emission pairs ($n-e$, $n+e$),

$$n / [(n-e) + (n+e)] = 0.5.$$

**Conjecture 6 (Wave Resonance in Emissions):**  
Let $e(n)$ be the set of all minimal even emission values for odd n as in Conjecture 1.
Then, the most frequent values of $e(n)$ in any sufficiently large interval are integer multiples of 6.

**Conjecture 7 (Recursive Predictive Emission):**  
Given a list of all primes less than n, define for each $p_i < n$, the candidate $C_i = 2n - p_i$.
Then, for every odd $n > 1$, at least one $C_i$ is prime for some $p_i$, guaranteeing the forward progression of the emission process.

**Conjecture 8 (Generalized Simplex Unsieveability):**  
Let $k ≥ 2$. For all sufficiently large primes p, there does not exist a sequence of k consecutive positive integers whose sum equals p.

**Conjecture 9 (Geometric Unsieveability):**  
There exists no finite geometric closure operation (based on simplex or higher-dimensional analogues) that can "cover" the set of all primes beyond a finite exceptional set.
